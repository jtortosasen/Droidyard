## Delegated decisions

- D47. **One version**: step 2 moves `0.18.0` → `0.19.0`; steps 4 and 5 ride on it.
- D48. **Bounds** (named constants, overridable through `Options` for tests): at most 1,000 items a call; a `file.copy` item, and a delete's cross-volume copy, at most 20,000 entries and 1 GiB, else that item fails `FILE_TOO_LARGE` with nothing touched; a same-volume delete has no size limit (one rename; its `bytes` and `entries` come from a walk capped at 20,000 entries and say so when cut). A workspace's trash keeps entries 7 days and at most 2 GiB, the oldest removed first, never the newest entry; the Undo stack 20 operations.
- D49. **Free names** (`onCollision: "keepBoth"`, on `file.move` and `file.copy`): a move takes `name 2.ext`, `name 3.ext`…; a copy `name copy.ext`, `name copy 2.ext`…; the extension is a file name's last dot-suffix unless the name starts with its only dot (`.gitignore copy`); a folder has none. At most 1,000 tries, then `FILE_EXISTS`.
- D50. **Trash ids** are 16 lowercase hex characters, a schema `pattern`, checked in the handler before any path is built: a malformed id fails the whole call `REQUEST_INVALID_PARAMS` (a lexical refusal, Q1); a well-formed id this workspace's trash does not hold, another workspace's included, is the item's `FILE_NOT_IN_TRASH`. The recorded `path` is resolved again through `resolve` and the `os.Root` at restore.
- D51. **Selection and keys.** Click selects one row; ⌘-click toggles; ⇧-click and ⇧↑/⇧↓ extend from the anchor over the visible rows; the tree is `aria-multiselectable`. While the tree has focus (never in a text field): ⌘X, ⌘C, ⌘V, ⌘⌫ (delete), ⌘Z (undo), F2 (rename). No ⌘D (Chrome bookmarks) and no ⌘N (Chrome's new window): Duplicate, New file and New folder are in the context menu (Shift+F10 opens it). An operation acts on the top-most selected paths (a selected descendant of a selected folder is dropped). A compacted row acts on its deepest folder (`row.path`); Rename edits that folder's name.
- D52. **Clipboard**: per project view, in memory, never the system clipboard; cut rows dimmed. Paste goes into the selected folder, or a selected file's parent. A copy pasted into its own folder is a duplicate (`keepBoth`); a cut pasted moves and empties the clipboard, and a cut pasted back into its own folder makes no call. Paste is disabled in another project (`file.*` paths are one root's).
- D53. **Drag** uses pointer events, not HTML5 drag and drop (which would need the Finder-drop handling the owner left out, and loses its source when a virtual row unmounts while scrolling): it starts after 4 px with the primary button; the target is the folder under the pointer or a file row's parent; a folder into itself or a descendant, or anything into its current parent, is no drop; a collapsed folder expands after 600 ms under the pointer; within 24 px of the viewport's top or bottom the list scrolls; ⌥ held at the drop copies; Escape cancels. Listeners sit on the window for the drag's life.
- D54. **Collisions in the UI**: items that come back `FILE_EXISTS` open one dialog naming them (text): Keep both (the same call for those items with `keepBoth`) or Skip. No Replace (Not in this milestone).
- D55. **Undo**: the project view's last 20 operations, memory only. A delete's inverse is `file.restore` of its trash ids; a move's or rename's, a move back of its done items; a copy's, duplicate's or create's, a delete (to the trash) of what it made. ⌘Z or the notice's Undo button; an inverse's own per-item failures are reported, nothing is forced; no redo. A reload, a project switch or a daemon restart drops the stack (a restarted daemon refuses the old per-launch token, `http-websocket.md` §Security baseline 4, so the tab must be opened again); the trash entries stay, and `file.restore` by id from the CLI or an agent still works (Q2).
- D56. **No confirmation before a delete** (it goes to the trash). One text line under the tree reports the last operation ("Deleted 3 items · 1 failed: …") with Undo, until the next one.
- D57. **Open tabs** follow this view's own moves, renames, drags and undone moves, for their done items (a folder by path prefix, never a sibling sharing a prefix); a tab whose file another client moved or deleted keeps m2d's deleted mark.
- D58. **Create** makes missing parents (as `file.write`). An inline name field at the row's depth: Enter commits, Escape cancels, blur commits; an empty name, `.`, `..`, or one holding `/` or NUL is refused inline with no call. A new file opens in a preview tab; a new folder is selected and expanded. A new or renamed path is selected and revealed when the watch reports it.

## Design questions

### Q1. Which capabilities do the tree's operations get, and do agents get them?

- Blocks: steps 2–7
- The documents say: `file.*` is list, read, write, watch (`mvp.md` table); delete, rename and mkdir are deferred (register row 137); `vision.md` §Principles #1 allows one exception, human-only policy (ADR-0015 §6), and ADR-0045 §4 added `workspace.chooseRoot` for "a window on the user's screen"; nothing on collisions, batches or new codes.
- Draft decision, a new ADR: five workspace-scoped commands, **audience all**, MCP tools under `all` and not `core` (ADR-0036 Decision 4), no trust check and nothing marked untrusted (ADR-0015 §1, §5): `file.create {path, type: file|dir}` → `{path, type}` (idempotent; not destructive: never replaces); `file.move {items: [{from, to}], onCollision?: fail|keepBoth}` → `{items: [{from, to, code?, message?}], done, failed}`, rename being a move inside one folder (idempotent; not destructive: never replaces, a move back undoes it); `file.copy`, same shape (long-running; not idempotent; not destructive); `file.delete {paths}` → `{items: [{path, trashId?, code?, message?}], done, failed}` (long-running; idempotent; destructive: the trash forgets, Q2); `file.restore {trashIds}` → `{items: [{trashId, path?, code?, message?}], done, failed}` (long-running; idempotent; not destructive; ids per D50). At most 1,000 items. **Batches.** Lexical refusals (outside the root, the root itself, `from` equal to `to`, a target inside its source, a NUL, a malformed trash id) fail the whole call before anything changes; what only the filesystem can say fails its item, beside the others, and the call **completes** with every item's outcome, all failed included, as a failed build completes. This adds a case to `mcp.md`'s errors-versus-failures list: a batch with failed items is a successful MCP call, and its text rendering opens with the failures and their codes so an agent cannot miss them. A descriptor's `errors` lists the call-level codes; an item's `code` is an enum of the codes an item can carry (each step's Schema line lists them). **Cancel** (extending ADR-0020 §States, as m1f D21 did, without superseding it): a cancel stops between items and inside a copy; with no item attempted the operation ends `cancelled`; otherwise it completes, the unattempted and interrupted items `OPERATION_CANCELLED`. A workspace close's forced cancel ends it `cancelled` (what was done stays done; trashed items stay in the trash). **No operation replaces an existing entry**: renames use the kernel's no-replace rename on descriptors the `os.Root` opened, and a volume that cannot refuse a replace fails the item rather than fall back; `keepBoth` asks the daemon for a free name. New codes `FILE_EXISTS`, `FILE_NOT_IN_TRASH`, `FILE_TOO_LARGE`. No resource lock: like `file.write`, an operation never waits for a build. Each call is one operation record; the trash's `entry.json` records what was deleted, from where and when.
- Alternatives considered: **audience human** (a third exception): neither of the two reasons the exceptions rest on applies (the agent authorising itself; a window on the user's screen), the agent's shell already moves files, and through the daemon an agent's delete becomes contained, observable and undoable by the human; it would save about one `tools/list` entry's bytes per capability. **One item per call, the UI looping**: one operation, Activity block and trace line per file, and no unit to undo. **The first failure as the call's error, done items in `details`**: `details` has no schema (register row 227) and the UI needs the done items for Undo. **A call error when every item failed**: two shapes for one outcome, and the collision dialog needs every failed item, which only the result carries. **A cancelled batch always `cancelled`**: its done items, which Undo needs, would be lost with the result. **A check, then `Root.Rename`**: a writer racing the check is overwritten, and `rename(2)` silently replaces an empty folder. **`onCollision: "replace"`**: an overwrite destroys; the UI can compose it undoably later. **`file.write` with an exclusive flag for create**: no folders. **A separate `file.rename`**: the same primitive.

### Q2. Where does a deleted item go, and what can Undo restore?

- Blocks: steps 5–7
- The documents say: ADR-0021 §Location keeps state out of the project, and ADR-0046 §4 (which brought only the project file in) keeps "everything else" out; ADR-0021 §Layout lists `ws/<wsid>/` without a trash; nothing on deleting project files.
- Draft decision, an ADR superseding ADR-0021 §Layout in part (it adds `ws/<wsid>/trash/`): a deleted item goes to `<state>/ws/<wsid>/trash/<id>/item` (D50's ids), its `entry.json` (`schemaVersion` 1: path, type, deletedAt, bytes, entries, aside) written first. On the state root's volume it is one `renameat`, any size. Across volumes (`EXDEV`) the item is first renamed aside in its own folder, so nothing written to its path afterwards is taken, then copied with every file and folder `fsync`ed (ADR-0021 §Format), and the aside removed only once the trash holds it whole; such a copy is bounded (20,000 entries, 1 GiB, else `FILE_TOO_LARGE` and the item stays). A symlink goes as the link. Entries go after 7 days, and the oldest first (never the newest) while a workspace's trash exceeds 2 GiB, swept at each delete and when the workspace opens. `file.restore` puts an item back at its recorded path, re-resolved inside the root (parents re-made, never replacing). The trash outlives a daemon restart, so `file.restore` by id still works; the tab's Undo stack does not (a restarted daemon refuses the old token). Deleting `ws/<wsid>/` (a workspace reset) empties it.
- Alternatives considered: **the system Trash** (`~/.Trash`): macOS guards it (`ls ~/.Trash` from a shell: "Operation not permitted", 2026-10-11), Finder's Put Back needs Finder's own metadata (AppleScript with an Automation prompt, or cgo), and Linux's trash is another specification; **a trash inside the project** (`.droidyard/trash/`): its strength is that every delete would be a same-volume rename, but ADR-0046 §4 keeps everything but the project file out of the tree (ignoring, read-only checkouts), and the watch, Git and the build would see it; **refusing cross-volume deletes** (an item failure, as a cross-volume move): smaller, and likely unused on the owner's Mac (Unverified), but a project on an external disk could not delete from the tree at all; **a trash beside the project on its own volume**: writes outside both the root and the state directory; **permanent delete with a confirmation**: the owner asked for a trash with Undo; **an in-memory undo copy**: lost with the daemon, and unbounded memory.

## Changes

Changes after approval: date, what changed, why, and the ADR if the change touches the design.

- 2026-10-11, step 2 (Delegated): `file.move` is **not** idempotent, against Q1's draft: the step verifier showed a swap batch `[a→t, b→a, t→b]` run twice swaps back, so a client retrying after a timeout would undo it. `file.create` stays idempotent. ADR-0053 does not name the hint; the descriptor carries it.
- 2026-10-11, step 3 (Delegated): D58's "blur commits" except an empty or unchanged name, which a blur cancels with no call and no message (Enter on an empty name shows the message); a failed commit keeps the field open with its message. `FileTreeModel.follow` carries D58's "selected and revealed when the watch reports it".
- 2026-10-11, step 4 (Delegated): `file.copy` allows `from` equal to `to` (D52's duplicate is a copy onto its own path with `keepBoth`; without it the item is `FILE_EXISTS`), against Q1's lexical list, which stays for `file.move`. A copy's walk skips droidyard temporary names. The bytes copied are held to the item's cap as they are read, not to each file's walked size (the verifier's finding).
- 2026-10-11, step 5 (Delegated): `entry.json` gains an optional `truncated` (a same-volume item's counts cut at the cap, D48); a cross-volume delete takes its copied snapshot from the aside after the rename and removes the aside only if a re-walk still matches it (names, kinds, sizes, modification times), else puts the original back and fails the item; the record is synced up to `ws/<wsid>/`, and the aside is not removed when `<id>`'s sync fails; restore removes a recorded aside and finishes a cut delete; codes outside an item's published enum fold to `FILE_IO_FAILED`. The verifier's two rounds.
- 2026-10-11, step 6 (Delegated): create and rename record Undo too (D55's inverses); a paste mixing items for other folders and duplicates is two calls (`onCollision` is per call); a multi-selection survives the deletion of one of its rows; a Keep both retry's own `FILE_EXISTS` is a plain failure, so the dialog cannot loop.
- 2026-10-11, step 7 (Delegated): ⌘ keys by `metaKey` only (Ctrl is not ⌘, as before); a right-click on a selected row keeps the selection; a drag from an unselected row selects it and carries it alone; the menu's Paste and Undo states are read when it opens.
- 2026-10-11, close (Delegated): create and rename write the notice line too, so its Undo always names the top of the stack (the close verifier's B1); a folder inside a compacted chain is selected and acted on as its row.
